The Beginning

Why I Started Building

Not from frustration with a coaching center. From frustration with a system that made preparation harder than it needed to be.

NEET preparation in India has always had a problem that nobody talks about openly — the infrastructure of information is broken. Resources are scattered across dozens of websites, YouTube channels, PDFs, and Telegram groups. There's no single place where everything is structured, verified, and free.

When you search for NEET content online, you're met with paywalls, misleading claims, outdated PDFs, and platforms so cluttered with ads that actually studying becomes secondary. Students spend more time navigating resources than using them.

NEETopia started as an attempt to solve a system problem using technology — one clean platform, structured like it should have always been, with NCERT at the center, free video lectures embedded where they're needed, and PYQs organized by chapter rather than hidden behind a login.

Not a coaching institute. Not a startup with investors. Just a student applying what he learned about code to something that actually matters.

Expensive coaching systems
Quality NEET preparation costs lakhs per year — putting it out of reach for millions of students.
Scattered, noisy resources
50 different websites, PDFs you can't trust, videos buried under three levels of playlists.
Misinformation online
Wrong answers, outdated syllabus info, and "guaranteed tips" that contradict NCERT.
No clean, structured platform
Nowhere that has syllabus, chapters, PYQs, study plans, and video lectures — all in one place.

The Process

Building While Learning

No computer science background. No professional training. Just YouTube, consistency, and the stubbornness to figure things out.

Phase 1
Learning web development from scratch
Started with zero formal training — HTML, CSS, JavaScript from YouTube. Watched tutorials, broke things, rebuilt them. No bootcamp. No course certificate. Just building until it worked.
Phase 2
Structuring and verifying NEET content
Every chapter cross-checked against NCERT. Every PYQ solution verified. Content accuracy matters more than shipping fast — wrong information on a medical entrance platform isn't just bad design, it's harmful.
Phase 3
Balancing college exams and late-night builds
5–6 months of building alongside a B.Sc CSDA curriculum at IIT Patna. Debugging responsive layouts after submitting assignments. Writing Biology notes at 1am. The platform was built around a college schedule, not the other way around.
Phase 4
Experimenting with AI — and learning what it can't replace
AI tools helped accelerate content writing and UI polish. But every fact still needs a human to verify it. The most important lesson: AI is a tool, not a substitute for knowing your domain.
Today
Still building. Still iterating. Completely alone.
No team. No funding. No co-founder. Design, development, content, SEO, testing — all one person. Every improvement on NEETopia is shipped by someone who is also studying for end-semesters.

The Work

What Happens Behind the Screens

A platform that looks clean on the surface takes a lot of invisible work to maintain.

Writing & structuring content
Every chapter's notes written from scratch — structured to match the NCERT flow while being easier to actually read and retain.
Cross-checking & verifying information
Every fact, every answer, every explanation cross-referenced with NCERT and trusted sources before it goes live. No rushing past this step.
Optimizing pages & SEO
So students searching for help actually find this platform. Meta tags, semantic HTML, page speed — the invisible infrastructure that makes discovery possible.
Fixing responsiveness issues
Most students access this on a phone, not a laptop. Every layout gets tested and adjusted until it works cleanly on every screen size.
Managing performance
Pages that load fast on slow internet connections. Optimized assets, minimal bloat. Because not everyone has a good Wi-Fi connection when they're studying.
Organizing PYQs
2013–2024 questions sorted by year, subject, and chapter. Not just dumped in a PDF — actually structured so they're useful for practice.
Improving UI/UX continuously
Every card, spacing value, and interaction tweaked based on what makes reading and studying feel less like a chore and more like flow.
Debugging — always debugging
Late nights finding out why a component breaks on Safari. Why a font doesn't render correctly. Why a grid collapses on 375px. The invisible hours.
